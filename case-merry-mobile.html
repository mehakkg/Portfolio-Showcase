01 · THE BUSINESS

A B2B product whose revenue depends on hospitals actually using it.

Merry Health connects about 30 Tier-2 and Tier-3 private hospitals (200+ beds) with ambulance partners, and 50–60% of rides are hospital-to-hospital transfers. A ₹3,000/month subscription failed on price, so the model moved to ₹200 per ride. Every request that skips the system is revenue lost.

GOALS
01More rides booked on-platform
02Clean, timestamped data for billing and SLAs
03More hospitals signed by proving reliability

02 · THE GAP

The workflow on paper and on the ground were two different systems.

WHY IT HAPPENED → WHAT IT COST
Staff are on the move, rarely at a desk→ Ops became a 24×7 human integration layer
WhatsApp needs no learning→ Reports and invoices were only as good as manual entry
No regulation requires digital tracking→ The dashboard became a month-end report, not a live tool
Two incidents that shaped the data model
01A 130 kg patient on a third floor with no lift. Lift, floor and stretcher-fit became booking fields.
02An ambulance sent without oxygen after a verbal mix-up. Equipment became an explicit selection.

06 · STRATEGY

Adoption before automation.

The first idea was AI dispatch: record the call, transcribe it, auto-fill and auto-assign. It broke in the vision call. It needed phone-system integration, drivers distrusted automated calls, and a voice note can’t give a pickup pin. The product had to absorb behaviour before replacing it.

[ADD: one line on which direction went forward and why.]

Three decisions, signed off by Vedang, [his role].

1WhatsApp stays the front doorFighting it had already cost the product its adoption.
2The dashboard becomes the recordEvery update, from any channel, lands in one timestamped source of truth.
3A manual fallback in every critical journeyWhen GPS, network or a driver fails, a human path exists.

07 · WORKFLOW & DECISIONS

Designing for the worst hour: five rides live at once.

Parallel emergencies, updates scattered across chats and calls, families asking “where is the ambulance?” The admin has to spot the ride in trouble, act on it, and close every trip with clean data.

Six concepts per screen, one hybrid, the trade-off stated.

2.0 · ACTIVE RIDESA priority list that sorts itself.Rides cluster into Critical, At Risk and On Track; silence or a slipping ETA moves a ride up.TRADE-OFFOverrides chronological order and needs thresholds tuned with ops.
2.1 · RIDE DETAILSRide health in two seconds.A health summary on top (status, ETA, driver state, halts), then the live map and event timeline, with alerts that suggest the next action.TRADE-OFFDepends on reliable GPS and needs firm visual hierarchy to avoid density.
2.1.1 · MAP VIEWPlanned vs actual route.Deviations are highlighted, with labels like “No movement 3+ min” or “Off-route”, and a mini timeline so context isn’t lost while zoomed in.TRADE-OFFHigher engineering effort for route and traffic data.
2.2 · DELAY ALERTRoot cause first, then the action.The panel says why (a halt, a network drop, an ETA jump), puts three actions up front, and codes severity so minor delays stay quiet.TRADE-OFFFalse alerts erode trust, so thresholds must be conservative.
3.0 · HANDOVERIts own step, not a field on a page.Tracking is for watching, handover is for verifying. A dedicated panel runs geofence arrival with a checklist fallback.TRADE-OFFOne extra step, kept because it creates the timestamp billing depends on.
4.0 · TRIP CLOSUREThe system checks before the human signs.Auto-validation flags missing timestamps, conflicting arrival data and bulk-closure patterns. A trip can’t close until its record is complete.TRADE-OFFRequires standardised status events from the driver side.

08 · THE EXPERIENCE

Built for an admin with a phone in one hand.

09 · OUTCOME

An agreed roadmap, and a measurable definition of success.

I closed with a 20-minute strategy pitch and a hi-fi walkthrough. Because the real problem was adoption, not a better dashboard, I aligned the client on four KPIs:

01 · ADOPTIONRequests via dashboard or WhatsApp automation~5% baseline → ≥70% gate
02 · OPERATIONSRequest-to-pickup timeOn time = under 20 min
03 · RELIABILITYTracking-link delivery to patient families
04 · ENGAGEMENTWeekly active hospitalsBase of ~30 hospitals
BEFOREChats, calls, paper notes, and a dashboard filled in after the fact.
DESIGNED FOROne record fed by the channel admins already use.

What I worked within, and what I’d keep.

CONSTRAINT ↳ HOW I HANDLED IT

A distributed team and late client inputs↳Workflow maps as the shared language
Feature phones and unreliable GPS↳A fallback for every automated step

REFLECTIONI could have designed the smartest dispatch system. I designed the one hospitals would actually use first.
01Fixing screens wouldn’t have moved adoption. Changing the workflow could.
02In emergency software, the real competitor is WhatsApp. You can’t out-feature it, so you integrate with it.
03A team moves fastest when each person owns a slice end to end. Mine was the hospital admin.
