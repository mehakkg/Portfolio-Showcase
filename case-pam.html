01 / 06

The problem

Feature-complete on paper. Admins got lost in the first hour of a POC, so deals stalled.

The product had vaulting, session recording, access policies, discovery and MFA. On paper it competed with CyberArk and Delinea.

Then a prospect's admin opened it: 14 ungrouped nav items, workflows split across sections, failures that happened quietly. Our own SEs sometimes asked me where features were.

FIG. 1 — THE ORIGINAL PRODUCT, ANNOTATED the pieces were all there. nothing held them together.

02 / 06

The audit

Before touching a screen, I audited 10 real admin scenarios. 140 issues traced back to 4 root causes.

The instinct was to redesign the worst screens. I pushed back for two weeks. Scenarios came from CS call recordings and support tickets: day-one onboarding, break glass during a Sev 1, rotating 200+ credentials, the audit review before a compliance call.

RC1

Fragmented information architecture

14 flat nav items. You had to know the internal object model to find anything.

RC2

Broken domain terminology

New admins spent their first week translating the interface.

RC3

No workflow continuity

JIT, approvals and break glass were missing or scattered.

RC4

Silent failure states

Errors weren't surfaced. Admins couldn't tell what broke.

The two deal-breakers: day-one onboarding (a new admin couldn't get to a working session in under 30 minutes without calling support) and break-glass elevation during a Sev 1 (the flow required knowledge of 3 separate sections that didn't surface together). Every other issue compounded from these two.

03 / 06

One filter

I replaced the RICE vs Kano debate with one yes/no question. A big slice of the backlog was deferred in two days.

THE ONLY QUESTION

Does this directly strengthen one of the five deal-breakers?

Vault & Rotation Discovery & Inventory Session Proxying Access Governance Integrations & Audit
Credential vaulting with rotation✓ Yes
JIT access with approval workflow✓ Yes
Custom dashboard themingDeferred
Stack bot notificationsDeferred

three sprints in, the PM called it the most useful prioritization tool we'd used.

04 / 06

The system

Three structural decisions: nav by intent, work that stays inside the resource, onboarding with checkpoints.

The old nav was organised by database object: System Users, Groups, Certificates, Policies. The new one is organised by what the admin is trying to do. Break glass lives under Security Ops, findable on day one without the docs.

14 → 5
Flat items → Intent groups
before, by object → after, by intent

Resources used to be a database view. Now each resource has its own workspace: credentials, sessions, approvals, and audit history all live together. Admins never need to jump across nav sections to complete a workflow.

everything you need, inside the resource

New admins previously had no guided path. We added a five-checkpoint onboarding flow that maps to real setup milestones — connect a resource, vault the first credential, create an access policy — so admins know where they are and what's left.

06 / 06

Lessons

In security products, usability is a security control.

The audit was the whole project

Every decision after was implementing conclusions we'd reached together.

Frameworks are conversations

RICE and Kano failed because the team used them to avoid talking about values.

WHAT I GOT WRONG

Migrating customers skipped onboarding stages. A persona is incomplete without knowing what they're migrating away from.
