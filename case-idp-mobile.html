02 · WHY IT MATTERED

Enterprise buyers evaluate at the seams, not on the feature list.

Three things were actually at stake.

01Misconfiguration is the threat model.An admin who picks SMS because the page didn't say it's interceptable has just weakened the organisation's authentication floor, and nothing in the product will tell him. Design decisions here are security decisions.

02A system that can't explain itself can't be sold into regulated industries.SOC 2 and ISO 27001 audits ask you to reconstruct a specific decision from four months ago and show your reasoning. We could show outcomes, not reasoning.

03The policy model couldn't represent reality.One group, one policy is clean until a contractor is also an engineer, which in a real company is most senior people. Every surface built on top inherited the gap.

03 · SYSTEM BEFORE SCREEN

I draw the system before I draw the screen.

habit 01 of 4

Before any Figma file, I mapped what depends on what. The team had been arguing about build order for a month. The map ended it in one meeting: the policy engine had to be right first, because everything else either feeds it or reports on it.

04 · FIX WHERE IT BREAKS

I fix things where they break, not where they show up.

habit 02 of 4

Two groups, two valid policies, nothing to decide between them. My first instinct was a conflict dialog: show both, let the admin pick. That's a fix at the point of pain, not the point of failure. By then someone's already locked out.

MY FIRST INSTINCTPOINT OF PAIN
1Admin writes rules
2Two policies collide
3Conflict dialog: pick one
too late: someone's already locked out
WHAT SHIPPEDPOINT OF FAILURE
1Choose the groups a rule targets
2Then write conditions
3No collision to resolve
the system knows who a rule covers before any condition exists

So scope moved to the front of the flow. You choose which groups a rule targets before you can write a condition. The collision can't happen, so there's nothing to resolve.

FIG. 3 · THE POLICY BUILDERScope first. You pick groups before you can write a condition, with ordered rules, a locked default and a live count of matching users.

05 · SIGNED-OFF IS STILL OPEN

I treat signed-off decisions as still open.

habit 03 of 4

That policy model had already been through review. I wrote up how it failed, built the alternative, and took it to the Head of Architecture and the POs. Uncomfortable meeting, right outcome. It changed before anyone built on it.

AS REVIEWEDSIGNED OFFOne group, one policy.
↓ wrote up how it failed, built the alternative
AS BUILTCHANGED BEFORE BUILDRules scoped to the groups they target.
ACROSS THE PROJECT8 to 10decisions went the same way.None of them because I pushed. All of them because the failure case was undeniable once someone drew it.

06 · MY OWN QUESTION

I check whether I'm answering my own question.

habit 04 of 4

I interviewed twelve teams about onboarding, then designed a fourteen-question form. People abandoned it. Cut to eight. Still wrong. Five.

FIG. 4 · QUESTIONS IN THE ONBOARDING FORM, ONE SQUARE EACH
ROUND 114People abandoned it.
ROUND 28Still wrong.
ROUND 35Back to my own notes.
SHIPPED3Produces a number: 2 of 7.
WHAT I HAD MISSEDI'd been solving data collection. The problem was sequencing.Three rounds in, I went back to my own notes and found the finding I'd been skimming: admins had no definition of finished. My answer had been to add a step before the sequence even began.

What shipped is three questions that produce a number: 2 of 7. Seven is what done means.

07 · HOW I KNOW IT WORKED

The strongest evidence is a failure with no incident history.

The multi-group lockout was caught and fixed before implementation. No migration, no affected customers, nothing to point at.

That's what a good architectural call looks like from the outside: nothing.

WHAT I CAN POINT AT
  • →A quarter of engineering work unblocked once the policy model settled.
  • →Eight to ten decisions reversed before build.
  • →The three-tier authentication structure is still the architecture of that surface, not a visual pass that got reworked.
  • →Every finding from the twelve interviews maps to something that shipped.
WHAT I DON'T HAVECompletion rates on the three-question flow against the fourteen-question one. The iteration was testing rounds, not instrumented funnels, so it's directional.
FIG. 6 · TIERED AUTHENTICATION METHODSStill the architecture of that surface.
IF I RAN IT AGAINDefine the success metric at the start, not at the end.Time-to-first-enforced-policy was the number that mattered. I only worked that out after the thing was built.
