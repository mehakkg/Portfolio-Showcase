01 · THE PROBLEM

On paper it matched CyberArk and Delinea. In a prospect’s hands, it lost the deal in the first hour.

PAM decides who can touch a company’s most sensitive systems. When it fails, the cost is a breach, so buyers judge it on one question: can my team run this safely from day one?

We had every capability that mattered. Then a prospect’s admin logged in for a proof of concept and found 14 ungrouped nav items, workflows split across sections, and failures that happened without a word. Even our own sales engineers sometimes asked me where a feature lived.

04 · NAVIGATION BY INTENT

Organised by what the admin is doing, not by database object.

I redesigned in the order the audit set: fix the structure first, then rebuild the two journeys every admin depends on.

05 · FLOW A · ONBOARDING

Seven stages, each one verified.

Day one onboarding. Fixes RC3 and RC4.

THE OLD PATHSetting up identity alone meant 14 tasks across 4 sections. Role mapping was buried in the second scroll of the SSO config. A failed directory bind gave no reason, and the only way to test a synced login was hidden inside “Show Machine Scan Results.” A first-time admin needed a support call every time.
4 sections→1 guided flow
WHAT I EXPLORED
Patch each screen where it livesREJECTEDThe gaps were structural. One logical setup sat across four sections, and no screen-level fix could connect them.
One long setup wizardREJECTEDThe audit had already shown what happens when every field arrives at once. The LDAP form overwhelmed first-time admins.
Seven-stage onboardingCHOSENSetup broken into stages an admin can finish and verify one at a time, turning setup into the proof of concept itself.
THE SEVEN-STAGE MAP

02Discover & import resources5–15 MIN

Every stage ends in proof.

01 · DIRECTORYConnected: 142 users and 18 groups synced. A preview shows which role one real user would log in with.

06 · FLOW B · ACCESS

One resource, one place, one trail.

Access, from the resource. Fixes RC3.

THE OLD PATHGranting access to one resource meant creating it, attaching a credential, mapping a policy and assigning users, each in a different section. Current access couldn’t be viewed anywhere. Requests arrived only as notification-bell items, and an approved ticket couldn’t be revoked. Every grant was standing access.
4+ nav visits→1 resource record
WHAT I EXPLORED
Upgrade the notification-bell approvalsREJECTEDAccess governance was a notification, not a workflow. A better bell is still a bell.
A standalone access-request moduleREJECTEDRequests, resource config and audit history in three places would rebuild the same scatter.
The resource as the anchor, with JIT inside itCHOSENCredentials, policies, access and audit history all attach to the resource, so one task happens in one place.
THE DECISIONEvery resource gets its own workspace: credentials, policy, access, sessions and audit trail. Access is requested and approved inside the resource and expires when its window closes, so nothing stays standing by default. Recording and MFA show on the resource’s security posture. Break glass sits under Security Ops as its own governed path.
STATES I DESIGNED
approval pendingmulti-level approvalrevoking an approved grantaccess expiring when the window closesbreak glass with justification and post-incident review

07 · MAKING IT HOLD

Language admins already speak, failures they can see.

Navigation fixed where things live. These two fixes made sure admins understood what they saw, and knew the moment something broke.

RC2

Language admins already speak

I rewrote the labels around the words admins use in their own runbooks and audit reports, not the words in our database schema.

System Users→Credentials
Admins looked for server passwords under a label that sounded like people
Users→People
Clashed with System Users, the most common first-day mix-up
All Tickets→Tickets & Approvals
Access requests read as a support inbox, not a governance step
Rotator Instance→Admin Account
A developer label shown on every rotation setup
RC4

Failures you can see

Errors used to fail silently. Now errors and reconciliation issues show up inline, on the resource where they happened, with what to do next.

BEFOREA plain user list. Nothing says anything is wrong.
AFTER4 sync failures, counted, explained, with a next step.

09 · LESSONS

In security products, usability is a security control.

01The audit was the whole project.Every decision after it was implementing conclusions the team had already reached together. Two weeks of pushing back on screen redesigns saved months of redesigning the wrong screens.
02Frameworks are conversations.RICE and Kano failed because the team used them to avoid talking about what mattered. One honest question did more than two scoring models.
WHAT I GOT WRONGCustomers migrating from another tool skipped onboarding stages, because they already knew what they wanted to set up.A persona is incomplete without knowing what the person is migrating away from.
